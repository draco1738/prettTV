Rivi 0.13 has four of these built in (marked above): settle, ring and label fade, lift shadow, hold ring with the side menu. All four are on by default. This command switches a running Rivi to the mix ticked on this page, without rebuilding. Rivi remembers it.

The timings and curves in TileView.kt, ShadowView.kt and SideMenu.kt are the numbers used here. To change one, change it in both places, then measure with bench/benchmark.sh nav.

What "checked" means. For every theme and refinement the Android code it needs was written (colour filters, per-paint alpha, rotation, overshoot, the platform click sound, a solid-colour fading edge, a pre-drawn blur sprite) and compiled against Rivi's build settings with no libraries. That proves each one can be built. It does not prove it stays smooth on a TV: a laptop browser draws far faster. Anything that goes into the app still has to pass the frame benchmark on a device (bench/benchmark.sh nav).

Deliberately left out because they cannot be done cheaply or at all: live blur behind menus (needs Android 12 and is heavy on this graphics chip), animated or video wallpaper (the reason Projectivy measured as heavy as Google TV), layered parallax artwork (apps supply one flat banner), scrolling long labels and "breathing" focus rings (anything that moves at rest ends the zero-idle result).

Sizes, colours, the 1.08 scale and the 110 ms timing on the left come from Rivi's code. The tiles are placeholders unless you add your own (see the README next to this file).

Five moments can make a sound. Pick a starting set, then tune each one. Move around the screen on the left to hear them in context: ← → ↑ ↓, Enter, hold Enter, Esc. Push against an edge to hear the edge sound. Whatever you set here is also what "Navigation click" plays on the other tab.

Your settings. This text is the whole configuration. Keep it with the WAV files you export. You can also paste settings here and press Load.

How this would work in the app. The free option is Android's built-in navigation click, which follows the system sound setting but cannot be tuned. Custom sounds like these mean bundling one tiny sound file per moment and playing it with Android's low-latency sound player. Each "Save WAV" button below exports that file.

Checked: the player, loading the files and reading the current audio output all compile against Rivi's build with no libraries. Not checked: how late the click arrives on a TV. Over Bluetooth speakers a click can lag by a tenth of a second or more, so the app would switch sounds off on Bluetooth output. The files would add roughly 10 to 30 KB to the 66 KB app.
